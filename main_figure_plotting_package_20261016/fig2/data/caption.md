**Fig. 2 | Feature sharing and structured exploration across tasks.**
**a,** Task 1 per-dimension FEL space (24 patterns; 51 participants, P1). FEL, feature enrichment level, is the sum across rows of the largest feature count within each row: \(\mathrm{FEL}_d=\sum_{r=0}^{2}\max_k n_{d,k,r}\), where \(n_{d,k,r}\) is the number of stimuli with feature \(k\) of dimension \(d\) in row \(r\). FEL ranges from 3 to 9. Marker area is proportional to choice frequency, defined here as the total number of times each FEL pattern was chosen, pooled across participants; it is not the number of distinct participants. Colour shows relative choice order from early to late; gold rings identify dimension-isolating sampling (DIS), in which rows are feature-homogeneous along one dimension. The same linear projection of per-dimension FEL applies to all patterns. Dessert and Drink insets show illustrative DIS arrangements; the Non-DIS inset depicts an observed partially enriched arrangement with FEL=(5,7,3): D1 and D2 are enriched, but neither reaches the complete-isolation maximum of9. It links to the existing aggregate pattern rather than a separate individual-trial point. Food icons illustrate feature identity, not measured attention.
In a, "Visit order" is centred over its colour key. Colours rank FEL patterns
by their aggregated mean normalized within-participant visit ranks and
rescale these pattern ranks from Early to Late; they do not represent an
individual participant's trial number or choice frequency.
**b,** Highest-FEL choice rate, expressed as the human − random difference in percentage points (105/80 participants, Task 1/2). FEL, feature enrichment level. Two-sided Wilcoxon tests; within-task Benjamini–Hochberg correction.
**c,** Mean FEL over ten progress bins, as percentage of task maximum (9/4); bands, descriptive SEM across participant-phases/participants, without chance adjustment.
**d,** Fruit-enriched Task 1 rows and sofa-enriched Task 2 selections. In Task 2, DIS selections share a feature value along one dimension.
**e,** Illustrative same-dimension runs and coverage. Object icons identify dimensions; shades distinguish features. Task-specific endpoint rules are defined in Methods.
**f,** Participant endpoints. Dashed lines labelled Random show random-selection means. Two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests against the supplied reference; within-panel Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Task 1 n=50/51/54 per endpoint; Task 2 single-dimension-bout length n=35/38, coverage n=40/40 (Obs/NObs). Reference n=104/104/973/1,999, left to right; eligibility is defined in Methods. All valid endpoints contribute to box summaries and tests.
Boxes, median/IQR; whiskers, 1.5×IQR; diamonds, means; dots in h, participant units. Individual and outlier points are hidden in c, f and g. Stars use panel-specified P values: *<0.05, **<0.01, ***<0.001. Exact statistics are in Source Data.
Double slashes indicate an omitted zero-to-lower-bound interval.
Visible Cartesian axes span their first and last displayed major ticks;
outer view margins retain the original data and uncertainty ranges.

DIS-frequency axes labelled “% of DIS trials” display 100 × the corresponding source proportion, with uncertainty bounds on the same percentage scale. Frozen source data and statistical analyses are unchanged.

Former g/h report analyses have moved to Fig. 5a/b; current Fig. 2 contains a–f.

Panel f Task 2 values are normalized by fixed task constants: single-dimension bout length / 60 trials and dimensions explored / 5 dimensions. Random means use the same divisors. Task 1 retains within-dimension run length / 30 or 50 trials and within-bout dimension coverage / 3 or 4. The source endpoint definitions differ across tasks; matching normalized labels do not imply a directly comparable estimand. Saved rank-sum tests and significance labels are unchanged. In c, Mean FEL is displayed as a percentage of the task-specific maximum, with the unit stated here rather than in the axis label.
