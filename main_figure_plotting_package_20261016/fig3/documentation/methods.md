## Common exposure-based uncertainty

These are behavioral exposure proxies, not fitted DGEM latent attention weights. Counts start at zero and dimension uncertainty is the sum over features of `1 / (exposure_count + 0.1)`. Task 1 exposure is the proportion of each feature in each ordered row, weighted `[1, 0.5, 0.1]`, multiplied by `0.1`; Task 2 exposure is the fraction of the four selected objects containing each feature, multiplied by `0.1`. Counts update after each choice independently of outcome and with attention multiplier 1. Uncertainty is recorded before and after updating. The Task 1 source analysis retains P2 and P2-only, corresponding to 4D-E and 4D-NE, and excludes 3D-NE/P1. Each subject-phase is a unit. Task 2 retains Obs and NObs and treats a subject-group as a unit. All finite valid source inputs and the source DIS labels are retained. Task 2 malformed selections and missing trial/noisy-score rows are excluded by the original parser; recomputed DIS-label discrepancies are exported but the source labels remain authoritative for panels a–c.

## a: uncertainty and DIS over progress

Normalized progress is `(trial_position−1)/(n_trials−1)`. Each of ten bins contains per-unit means of pre-trial total uncertainty and DIS probability. The plotted center is the average of those unit-bin means; the pointwise normal 95% confidence half-width is `1.96 × SD/sqrt(n)` across available unit-bin means. The source clips the DIS interval to [0,1] for display. No statistical test of the temporal co-decline is supplied. Bin-specific unit and trial counts are frozen separately.

## b: DIS probability by within-trial uncertainty rank

For every trial with finite uncertainties on all 4/5 dimensions, uncertainties are sorted in ascending order. Rank 1 means lowest uncertainty and the last rank means highest uncertainty; exact ties are broken deterministically by dimension identifier, not averaged ranks. The indicator is 1 for a DIS choice in that rank's dimension, and 0 otherwise; non-DIS trials contribute zero to every rank. Thus this is an unconditional DIS selection probability, not the distribution of attended dimensions conditional on DIS. Probabilities are averaged within each unit, and plotted means and SEM use those unit probabilities. Later ranks are compared with rank 1 by paired one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (`alternative='greater'`, `zero_method='wilcox'`, SciPy default method); Benjamini–Hochberg correction is applied within each task's 3 or 4 comparisons. These supplied tests are exactly reproduced and frozen in `b_rank_statistics.csv`.

## c: all-DIS-trigger uncertainty trajectories (Figure 9 source)

The displayed trajectories use the requested window −3 to +3, without changing the stored ±5-trial arrays or event membership. The summary heading `Δ Max. dim. uncertainty` denotes Umax(0) − Umax(+3), retaining the original sign convention: positive values indicate a decrease. Exact viewport endpoints are an intentional boundary exception; continuous trajectories meet and are clipped by the −3/+3 view edges. No summaries or tests are recomputed for this display change.

Every DIS trial is a trigger, including DIS trials within an ongoing bout. Other triggers were sampled in the supplied Figure 9 analysis from non-DIS trials pooled across units in twenty normalized-progress bins, matching the DIS count in each bin. Sampling uses replacement only when candidates are fewer than required; source seeds are 20260915/20260916 for Tasks 1/2. Controls need not come from the same participant. If a bin has no candidates, its DIS count is not matched. The reconstruction copies the supplied event tables, without rerunning control selection. There are 1,516 triggers of each type in Task 1 and 1,849 of each type in Task 2.

Available pre-trial maximum dimension uncertainties in the ±5-trial window are retained even if a complete window is unavailable. Curves are averages and SEM over event windows, so contributors are event-weighted and repeatedly measured; they are not participant-bootstrap confidence intervals. For each eligible trigger with a +3 trial, the difference between the trigger's and +3 trial's maximum dimensional uncertainty is computed. Differences are averaged within unit and event type. The side plot is the mean ± SEM across these unit means. The source compares the two sets of unit means using two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, despite overlapping participants between sets; this exact source choice is preserved, not replaced with a paired test. The two task tests form one Benjamini–Hochberg family. Exact sample counts, U, raw/adjusted P and all unit reductions are frozen. This analysis is observational and maximum uncertainty decreases mechanically as the exposure counts accumulate; it does not establish that initiating DIS causally accelerates learning.

## Exploration-index progress (panel e; source alias d)

The reconstruction uses the frozen exploration-index columns supplied in the handover. Task 1 includes phases `P2` and `P2-only` and reads `EI_original`; Task 2 includes the `observation` and `non_observation` groups and reads `exploration_index`. Rows missing the corresponding index are excluded, exactly as in `plot_45_ei_progress.py`. This leaves 3,850 trials from 105 Task 1 participants and 4,031 trials from 80 Task 2 participants. Of these, 1,443 and 1,802 trials, respectively, are classified as DIS. Task 1 uses the supplied `FDS_pattern == FDS` label. For Task 2, the maximum feature multiplicity among the four selected stimuli is calculated for each of the five dimensions; DIS is the sorted pattern `41111`.

Within each task, the retained trials are pooled and divided into 12 equal-width bins of **absolute trial number** using `pandas.cut(trial, 12, include_lowest=True)`. The bin centre is displayed at `(bin + 0.5) / 12`. This matches the supplied figure and is not a within-participant normalization by each participant's game length. The pooled-trial mean index and its SEM are retained. The DIS proportion is calculated over the same EI-valid trial pool; its band is the binomial standard error `sqrt(P * (1 - P) / N)`. Consequently, the two bands have different definitions. Neither band is a participant-bootstrap interval, and neither should be presented as a participant-level confidence interval.

Mechanical verification of the frozen columns shows that Task 1 `EI_original` equals the Euclidean norm of the **previous** row's `explore_vec_original` within participant × phase, ordered by trial (3,850 valid rows; maximum floating-point error 7.11 × 10⁻¹⁵). It does not equal the norm of the same-row vector. For Task 2, `exploration_index` equals the Euclidean norm of the same-row `actual_vec - pred_vec` (4,031 valid rows; zero discrepancy). These checks establish consistency of the supplied columns, not the semantic construction of their feature vectors. The handover does not supply the vector-generation procedures, so no general learning-model formula is introduced and no alignment is changed.

The supplied Task 1 y range (12–27) clips the last two SEM bands: the minimum mean minus SEM is 11.2311. The supplied Task 2 range (4–4.75) clips one lower band and one upper band: the full band range is 3.98330–4.75170.
